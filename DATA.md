# Data

burnrate ships **no external datasets**. Every metric stream used by the
tests, benchmarks and demo is synthetic and generated at runtime by
`internal/sim` from the scenario definitions in `internal/sim/scenarios.go`.

| Data | Source | License | Size |
|---|---|---|---|
| OTLP traffic for 8 scenarios (`burnrate scenarios`) | generated by `internal/sim` (deterministic; optional seeded volume noise) | MIT (this repo) | not stored; ~368k samples per 4-day replay |
| Example SLOs `examples/slos/*.yaml` | written for this repo | MIT | < 4 KB |
| Generated rules `examples/prometheus/slo-rules.yaml` | output of `burnrate rules` (golden file) | MIT | < 20 KB |
| promtool unit test `examples/prometheus/slo-rules.test.yaml` | hand-written input series | MIT | < 4 KB |
| Fuzz seeds `internal/series/testdata/fuzz/` | produced by `go test -fuzz` | MIT | < 1 KB |

The latency profile (`sim.DefaultProfile`) and bucket bounds
(`sim.DefaultBounds`) follow the OpenTelemetry HTTP semantic-convention
default buckets for `http.server.request.duration`; the share of requests per
bucket is invented to give a plausible healthy service (99.5% under 250 ms).
Traffic volumes, error ratios and incident shapes are invented to exercise
specific alert paths. None of it is measured from a real system.

Reference material (not data, nothing copied): the multiwindow,
multi-burn-rate method and its parameters come from chapter 5, "Alerting on
SLOs", of *The Site Reliability Workbook* (Beyer et al., O'Reilly 2018).
